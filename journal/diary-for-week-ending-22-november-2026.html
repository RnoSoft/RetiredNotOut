Diary for the Week Ending 22nd November

22nd November 2026

Journal Posts: Previous

Canal Near Marsden on a Frosty Day in 2009

Back in April, I decided to write an online diary. So far, I’ve only written about the days when something interesting happened. The other day, I decided to write a diary post every Sunday to cover the ordinary things that happened. This post is the first of them. It covers the period from when I got back from Liverpool last October until today. A tad longer than a week, but it is the first one.

My New Website

Back in September, I decided to rewrite and redesign this website using the Astro Website Builder. I’m writing a series of posts on how I am doing this. The first post in the series is here.

Since my trip to Liverpool, I’ve been thinking about its look and feel. One thing I want to do is remove the journal menu item. With the current approach, whenever I add a new post, every page needs to be updated (to change the page journal menu item link). That’s just over 400 pages. Instead of a single list of journal posts, linked together. I’m going to have lists of specific post types. Such as a diary page listing my diary posts. A trips-away page listing all my trips. And so on. This is a work in progress. I’m still not sure about the final look and feel. In fact, I probably won’t be sure until it’s finished.

I started work on my database of internal and external links. One thing I want to be able to do in future is easily find out if a linked page no longer exists or has changed. This caused me a lot of pain and took up a lot of my time. I’ll be writing about this in a separate post, soon.

The photos on this website need almost a gigabyte of storage. I use GitHub to host my website. I’m becoming concerned they might tell me my website is too big to be hosted for free. Luckily, I’ve found a solution: moving the images to Cloudflare R2 (10 gigabytes for free). Many thanks to Emma Goto for this post on her approach to hosting website images. It helped me a lot.

I was hoping to have the new website up and running by Christmas. As usual, my timing is off; it’ll probably be the end of February. One thing I need to do is revisit every page of my website.

Walking

I’ve decided I need to get fitter. I need to be able to walk for longer periods of time. So, I’ve been out walking around the village more. Mainly along the canal for now. After the winter, I’m hoping to start walking around the hills here, one of the reasons I moved to Marsden.

Just because I’m seventy-one, I should not be knackered after walking for a couple of hours.

People run marathons at my age!

Reading

I’m currently reading two books.

The first is ‘I Deliver Parcels in Beijing’ by Hu Anyan. He always wanted to be a writer. With this book, his first, he has an international bestseller. It’s about the various jobs he had after leaving school. Delivering parcels, selling bikes and working in warehouses. He’s written about boring, mundane jobs and made them interesting. I’ve been waiting ages for it to come out in paperback.

The second is ‘True North (On the Road in Canada)’ by Paul Theroux (Louis Theroux’s dad). I first came across his travel writing in the seventies. I went on a weekend train trip from Crewe to the top of Scotland (tiring; we had to sleep on the train on Friday and Saturday night). One of our group was reading Paul’s first travel book, ‘The Great Railway Bazaar’. When I got back, I bought it and thoroughly enjoyed it. I’ve read every one of his travel books since. They are well worth reading if you like travel books.

It came out in Hardback on the 28th of September. I usually don’t wait for his books to come out in paperback; I wanted to read this one immediately. I pre-ordered it from Amazon (£16.99, reduced from £22). I’m now learning a lot about Canadians and Canada.

Sadly, at the start of the book, he says it will probably be his last travel book. He’s 85.

I’m told he is also an excellent novelist.

Trips Away

I’ve set up two trips for my wife and I.

Three nights in Llandudno in December, staying at the Travelodge for £84, no breakfast. Opposite the hotel there’s an interesting Weatherspoons; they sell a decent breakfast (and if you are that way inclined, a pint as well). I’m now working out where to visit, eat and drink. Eating out could be a problem due to office parties.

Earlier this week I came across what looks like an excellent Bed and Breakfast on the Alister Sawday site. It’s Hardwick House in Shrewsbury. Details here. A lot more expensive than the Travelodge, £130 a night, but it looks like a very, very nice place to stay. We’ve decided to give it a go. So, in mid-January, we’ve two nights at my sister’s in Liverpool, followed by a night in Shrewsbury.

Shrewsbury indoor market is one of the best in England (proper meat and cheese stalls). We are staying Thursday night, and plan to shop on Friday morning before heading home.

Christmas

Last Tuesday I popped into the village to get us a bottle of the falling down water (the red variety). Through a couple of windows, I saw Christmas Decorations. On Thursday we did our weekly shop; we saw several houses with Christmas decorations up outside.

We’re halfway through November, for Christ’s sake.

Other

The boiler timer started acting up; £160 for a new one. The firebricks in the log burner are on the way out; cost unknown. Yesterday I signed up for Apply TV, lots of very interesting (which means great acting and script) series to watch, starting with the excellent ‘Slow Horses’. Drinks in the usual pubs: The Wellington Inn, Greenfield; The Travellers Rest, Meltham; The Sportsman, Huddersfield; and the King’s Head, Huddersfield (note: this diary post covers over three weeks). Finally opened our joint account with the Nationwide.
